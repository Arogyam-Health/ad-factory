Ad format rules:

1. Hero

Meaning:
The product is the main character. The ad should make OK Kit look like the clear solution for the selected persona’s weight-loss problem.

Job of the ad:
Make the customer think: “This is the course I need.”

Copy style:
Direct, confident, product-first.

Internal writing logic:
Persona problem -> OK Kit as the solution -> practical benefit.

Avoid:
Do not make it sound like a generic brand poster.

2. UGC

Meaning:
The ad should feel like it comes from a real customer, creator, or everyday person, not from the brand.

Job of the ad:
Make the customer think: “Someone like me has this exact problem.”

Copy style:
Casual, lived-in, conversational. Slightly imperfect is fine. It should not sound overly polished.

Internal writing logic:
Real-life moment -> relatable struggle -> OK Kit feels followable.

Avoid:
Do not invent fake names, exact results, or fake personal stories.
Do not write fake influencer-style praise.

Bad:
“Obesity Killer Kit changed my life completely.”

Better:
“I mostly needed something I could actually follow for 15 days.”

3. Product-feature led

Meaning:
The ad explains one important feature of OK Kit and connects it to a real customer problem.

Job of the ad:
Make the customer think: “Now I understand how this helps.”

Copy style:
Clear, simple, educational, benefit-linked.

Internal writing logic:
Feature -> why it matters -> customer benefit.
Use FAB silently, but do not mention FAB.

Avoid:
Do not list features without explaining why they matter.

Bad:
“Includes liquid, tablet, powder, tracker, and coach support.”

Better:
“Liquid helps with cravings. Tracker and coaches help you stay consistent.”

4. Testimonial

Meaning:
The ad uses customer experience, proof, or result pattern as the main hook.

Job of the ad:
Make the customer think: “This has worked for people like me.”

Copy style:
Specific, believable, proof-led.

Internal writing logic:
Customer experience/proof -> reason it worked -> next step.

Avoid:
Do not invent names, exact kg loss, medical conditions, or direct quotes unless provided.
Do not claim every customer gets the same result.

Bad:
“Priya lost 5 kg with PCOD.”

Better:
“Customers often say the biggest change is that the course feels easier to follow.”

5. Before-after

Meaning:
The ad shows the shift from the customer’s current struggle to a better state after following the course.

Job of the ad:
Make the customer clearly see the transformation.

Copy style:
Contrast-led, simple, believable.

Internal writing logic:
Before struggle -> after shift -> bridge through OK Kit.
Use BAB silently, but do not mention BAB.

Avoid:
Do not promise guaranteed body transformation or unrealistic visual results.

Bad:
“From fat to fit in 15 days.”

Better:
“From confused dieting to a clear 15-day course.”

Concept angle rules:

1. Auto
Meaning:
If the user selects Auto, choose the most suitable angle based on the selected persona and format.
Rule:
Do not mention “Auto” in the output.

2. Pain point
Meaning:
Lead with the customer’s problem.

3. Desired outcome
Meaning:
Lead with the result the customer wants.

4. Social proof
Meaning:
Lead with other customers’ words, experiences, or proof.

5. Authority
Meaning:
Lead with doctor credibility, expertise, hospital backing, or Ayurvedic authority.

6. Story
Meaning:
Lead with a relatable customer moment, mini-narrative, or lived situation.

7. Curiosity
Meaning:
Lead with intrigue or a question that creates genuine interest.

8. Comparison
Meaning:
Lead with a contrast against common failed methods or alternatives.

9. Offer
Meaning:
Lead with the deal, course box, guarantee, kit value, or buying proposition.

Concept angle usage rules:
1. Use only one concept angle per batch.
2. The concept angle controls what the ad leads with.
3. The format controls how the ad is expressed.
4. The persona controls what must feel specific.
5. Do not make the copy sound like it is following a labeled framework.

Framework usage rules:
1. Do not ask me to choose PAS, BAB, FAB, or 4U.
2. Use these frameworks silently only as internal writing logic.
3. Do not mention PAS, BAB, FAB, or 4U in the output.
4. Use PAS only lightly for pain-led ads. Do not over-agitate weight, body image, or medical conditions.
5. Use BAB mainly for Before-after ads.
6. Use FAB mainly for Product-feature led ads.
7. Use 4U only as a headline quality check:
   - Is the headline useful?
   - Is it specific?
   - Does it feel relevant now?
   - Is it clear why this persona should care?
